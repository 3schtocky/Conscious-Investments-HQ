# Screening playbook: what we hunt

## A Gem is an inflection, not a bargain
We are looking for businesses where something is *changing for the better, visibly in the numbers, before the market has fully priced it*. Three things must be true together:
1. **Acceleration.** Revenue growth is speeding up: the latest quarter's year-on-year growth is clearly above the prior quarter's. Steady high growth is good; *increasing* growth is what we want.
2. **Operating leverage.** Margins are widening (gross margin first, operating margin when gross profit isn't reported). Growth that costs more each quarter is not an inflection.
3. **The market is starting to notice.** Six-month price momentum is positive. A great story with a falling stock needs a specific reason it will turn; without one, it is not ready.

The screen scores these as acceleration 35 percent, growth 25, margin 20, momentum 20. The score is a way to rank a long list quickly. It is never the decision: you are the second opinion that decides what a number means.

## What a Gem is not
- A one-off: a licensing milestone, a single big order, an acquisition that lifts one quarter, a settlement. (The screen caps the obvious cases; you catch the rest.)
- A commodity or rate-driven move: oil, gas, shipping, metals, a rate cut lifting a lender. The business did not improve; the price of its input or output did.
- A turnaround from a trough that merely returns to normal.
- A recent listing, a reverse split, or a thin, illiquid stock the screen's floors let through by a hair.
- A loss-maker living on borrowed time: the screen allows losses only with at least two years of cash runway. Treat two years as the minimum, and ask what dilution or debt maturity could shorten it.

## Core names
Core is the quality-compounder lane ($2 bn and up): value, quality, growth and momentum weighted 30/30/20/20. Judge it as a business you would be happy to own through a bad year: durable margins, returns on capital that hold up, growth that does not depend on one customer or one year. Core pitches must say why the multiple is not the whole story, but still give no price target.
