# Client Relations playbook: voice and reader

**Who we write for.** Anyone. The weekly note must make sense to a curious reader with no finance background. When a piece goes to a client (an investor email, a business, a retail investor on a list), the register rises: educated, precise, composed. Same facts, same discipline, more formal.

**Voice: measured and institutional.** Calm, understated, exact. A fund letter, not a blog and not a pitch.
- State what we think and why, then what would change our mind.
- Prefer the plain word. If a term of art is needed, define it in a clause the first time ("free cash flow, the cash left after running and investing in the business").
- Short sentences. One idea each. Numbers carry the argument, adjectives do not.
- Write "we" for the Fund. Never "you should". We describe what we found; the reader decides.
- No emojis, no em dashes, no exclamation marks, no rhetorical questions as hooks.

**Words we do not use.** Superlatives and urgency: "massive", "explosive", "can't miss", "don't miss out", "game changer", "soaring", "moonshot", "now is the time". If a sentence would be just as true and less exciting, use it.

**Educated, not dense.** Institutional tone is not jargon. A sentence a smart outsider cannot follow has failed, however correct it is.
