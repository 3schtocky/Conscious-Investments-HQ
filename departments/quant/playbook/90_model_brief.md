# Quant playbook: the Model Brief

When {captain} approves a model, Quant owes the office a Model Brief for that version. It is the one place other wings learn what the model means; Client Relations reads it and never the workbook.

**Code builds the facts** (`read_model_brief`): targets, what each valuation method says, the drivers ranked by how far they move the target, the sensitivity table and the simulation. Do not retype or re-derive them.

**You write the reading** (`save_model_brief`):
- **View.** One short paragraph: what the model says and why the rating follows from it.
- **Drivers.** The 3 to 5 inputs the thesis leans on, named exactly as the facts name them, each with why it matters to a reader, in plain English.
- **The three cases.** Bear, base and bull as stories a smart outsider can follow, not as lists of inputs.
- **Sensitivity.** What the table shows in a sentence or two: which assumption the target is most exposed to.
- **What would break it.** Observable things (a number, a quarter, an event) that would prove the model wrong. At least as specific as the upside is confident.

**Rules.** Quote only dollar figures the facts list; the check refuses any other. Say plainly when the model rests on one assumption (a terminal value that carries most of the DCF, a single driver) and repeat any model warning in your own words. Do not soften a weak spot to make the story cleaner. When the brief is saved and the report is finished, the office files the hand-off card itself.
